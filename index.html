Beyond the Eucalyptus: The Fight for Australia's Koalas

A visual story about where koalas live, what puts them at risk, and why reliable monitoring matters.

FIT3179 · DV2 · October 2026

THE BIG PICTURE

Finding koalas is difficult. Protecting their habitat is essential.

Koala numbers are estimated using different survey methods, while habitat maps and rescue records answer different questions. These thirteen visualisations (including an Australia locator) use the Queensland koala hospital records included with this project. Counts reflect reported cases, not all koalas in the wild.

Important context

The Australian Government's 2025 estimate for listed koala populations in Queensland, New South Wales and the ACT was 398,000–569,000. The government warns that this is not directly comparable with the 2024 estimate because survey coverage and methods changed. A larger estimate does not by itself prove population growth.

01 / WHERE THEY CAN LIVE

Reported koala encounters cluster near urban areas

Begin with an Australia overview to locate the study area. The three detailed maps then zoom into southeast Queensland, where most records in this dataset are concentrated. These are hospital records, not koala population estimates.

Australia overview · Where is the study area?

For readers unfamiliar with Australian geography: the highlighted area is southeast Queensland on Australia’s east coast. Maps 1–3 zoom into this region.

Map 1 · Proportional symbol map of hospital records

Circles represent local government areas, placed at their median recorded location; circle size represents hospital record counts, not wild koala populations. The detailed map zooms into southeast Queensland; use the Australia overview above for geographic context.

Map 2 · Bin map of recorded incidents

Equal-sized 0.22° grid cells show reported incidents. Orange marks fewer cases; darker blue marks more. A logarithmic scale makes smaller clusters easier to distinguish.

Map 3 · Flow map of rescues and recorded releases

A sample of records with an original and release coordinate 5–85 km apart. Teal circles show the original location and orange triangle arrowheads show the release end and movement direction. Lines show straight-line movement, not actual travel routes. Many shorter moves are omitted for legibility.

READ THE EVIDENCE

What these data can—and cannot—tell us

Population estimates

Modelled estimates depend on survey coverage and method. Compare across years only when the source confirms comparability.

Habitat maps

Suitability models identify likely habitat and priority areas; they do not confirm that koalas are present at every mapped location.

Rescue records

Hospital and rescue records describe reported animals and incidents. They are influenced by human access, reporting and rescue capacity.
