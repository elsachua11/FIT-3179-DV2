The big picture

Koalas live at the edge of Australia's biggest cities. That is the problem.

Malaysians know the tension between growing towns, new roads and wildlife, from Malayan tigers to tapirs. Koalas show the same tension on Australia's east coast. Their forests sit right beside suburbs, highways and dogs, and the numbers we have are hard to compare.

01 / How many koalas are there?

Nobody can count them exactly

Koalas live high in trees and spread across forests, so the figures are modelled estimates, not head counts. Start with where koalas are protected, then see how the estimates and the decline numbers compare.

Map 1 · Choropleth map

Where are koalas listed as endangered?

Each state is coloured by its national koala listing status. Orange identifies the Queensland, New South Wales and ACT population listed as endangered since February 2022.

So what? The listed population is only on the east coast, but Victoria and South Australia hold hundreds of thousands of koalas too and are not nationally listed.

Chart 2 · Range (floating bar) chart

Official estimates of wild koalas, 2021–2025

Each bar spans the low and high estimate for Queensland, NSW and the ACT. A wide bar means high uncertainty. The black tick is a single-number estimate with no range.

So what? The larger 2025 national estimate mainly reflects wider surveys and better detection, not confirmed population growth.

Chart 3 · Connected comparison

Koala declines have been serious across the east coast

Each line starts at zero and ends at the reported decline. New South Wales has a range; the other published figures are single estimates or minimums. These cover different time periods.

So what? All three reports describe substantial declines. They cannot be combined into one Australia-wide percentage.

Why it matters

Two threats behind the decline

Figures from the Australian Senate inquiry into koala conservation.

Habitat loss. At least 885,596 hectares of forest and woodland known or likely to be koala habitat were cleared between 2000 and 2016.

Bushfire. The 2019–20 "Black Summer" fires burnt more than 500,000 hectares of forest. Estimates of koala deaths range from at least 6,400 to about 61,000, so even the damage is uncertain.

So what? Habitat loss is slow and steady, while fire is sudden. Chart 15 places both on one timeline.

02 / Where do koalas live?

Habitat is strongest on the coast, and so are the people

Two views of the same story. First, survey results across New South Wales. Then a close look at south-east Queensland, where koalas share land with Brisbane, the Gold Coast and millions of people.

Map 4 · Grid choropleth

Where do surveys find koalas in New South Wales?

Each square is a cell about 10 km wide. Colour shows the share of surveyed sites in the cell where koalas were found, from the NSW Koala Likelihood Map (2019). Cells with fewer than 5 surveyed sites are hidden.

So what? Koalas are found most reliably along the north-east coast and in a cluster inland in the north. Pale cells mean few koalas were found where people looked.

Chart 5 · Treemap

Which councils send the most koalas to hospital?

Area is proportional to koalas admitted from each local government area, 1997–2024. Hover for exact counts.

So what? Moreton Bay and Redland, the two bayside councils around Brisbane, make up more than half of all admissions that have a mapped location.

So what? The hottest cell moved from south-east of Brisbane (1997–2005) to north of it (since 2006), and active cells more than doubled from 39 to 94.

Map 8 · Flow map

Where are rescued koalas released?

Each line joins the place a koala was found (circle) to the place it was released (triangle), when the two are 5–85 km apart. A random sample of 140 of 646 such records.

So what? Most koalas are released close to where they were found. Only about 8% of records with a release point (646 of 8,086) were released 5–85 km away. Lines are straight, not real routes.

03 / What puts koalas in hospital?

Disease, cars and the breeding season

The hospital records show what goes wrong for koalas that live near people. A koala can be flagged for more than one cause, such as sick and injured, so shares add up to more than 100%.

Chart 9 · Radial bar chart

Which months are busiest?

Each wedge is a month of the year. Longer wedge = more koalas admitted, added up over 1997–2024. Orange marks August to October.

So what? One in three admissions happens in just three months, when koalas are moving around to breed.

Chart 10 · Heat map

Is the busy season the same every year?

Each square is one month of one year. Darker orange = more koalas admitted. The black box marks the single busiest month.

So what? The August to October band shows up in almost every year. The busiest month, September 2019, came during a severe drought.

Chart 11 · Heat map

What are koalas admitted for?

Each square is the share of that year's admissions flagged with a cause. Darker = a bigger share. Numbers in the squares show selected years.

So what? Vehicle strikes, dog attacks and koalas found dead have all become a smaller share (vehicle hit: 36% to 17%), while sickness is the main reason, about 56% of admissions in 2024. Recording practice may have changed, so treat trends with care.

Chart 12 · Bar chart

Do faster roads kill more koalas?

Koalas hit by vehicles, grouped by the posted speed limit of the road (only recorded for some strikes).

So what? Strikes happen on every kind of road, but 80 km/h and above roads account for 55%. Counts are not adjusted for how much road exists at each speed.

Chart 13 · Mosaic chart

What happens after someone reports a koala?

Each column is a situation when the koala was found. The width of a column is its share of all reports. Colours show what happened next. Numbers are percentages of that column.

So what? Koalas found on the ground are usually taken to hospital (86%), while koalas seen in trees are as likely to be left alone (41%) as admitted (41%), because a koala in a tree is often fine.

04 / Has the response changed?

More protection and money

Conservation changed in two ways: governments raised the koala's legal status and funding, and the way koalas are recorded and counted changed too. Both matter when reading the charts above.

Chart 14 · Stacked bar chart (click the legend)

The hospital log changed over time

Records per year, split into koalas taken to hospital and other reports (sightings, animals left alone). Click a legend entry to highlight it.

So what? After 2016 almost every row is a hospital admission. Counting all rows would show a fake decline, so every trend on this page uses admissions only. Admissions stayed steady at about 900–1,700 a year, peaking at 1,731 in 2019.

Chart 15 · Conservation response timeline

When did conservation action change?

Read across the years: horizontal bands show pressures that lasted several years, while circles show dated government decisions and new surveys. The sequence highlights the change from documenting threats to making stronger commitments.

So what? Habitat loss and severe fires came before stronger protection. Endangered status in 2022 and later monitoring are responses—not proof that populations have recovered.

What can we say?

Koalas are in serious trouble in the places where they are listed, and they live closest to people on the Queensland coast. Disease, vehicles and the spring breeding season put the most koalas in hospital.

05 / Conclusion

What does the evidence tell us?

Koalas remain endangered in Queensland, New South Wales and the ACT. Improved monitoring is finding animals that earlier surveys missed, but this is not proof of population recovery. Hospital records reveal pressures from disease and vehicles in populated parts of Queensland, while habitat protection remains essential.

Population estimates
